Reading Overview, week 1

Reading: Reading: Donna Riley, “Mindsets in Engineering,” in Engineering and Social Justice (Springer International, 2008), 33-45.

Overview: The reading for this week is from a book by engineering professor Donna Riley, who argues that engineers should focus more on social justice than supporting the status quo. In the chapter I’ve assigned from the book, Riley talks about the “mindsets” of engineering, attitudes held by engineers and by others about engineers. These mindsets stress problem-solving, without regard to whether solving the problem will “improve the human condition,” as called for in Georgia Tech’s mission statement.

The Riley reading is short, about 11 pages, but also pretty dense. Focus on the key ideas.

Questions to think about while reading:

What positive aspects of the engineering mindset does Riley identify?
What negative aspects does she discuss? (She finds more negative than positive).
Do the jokes accurately reflect your view of engineers, either from the perspective of an engineering student or a student majoring in a non-engineering field?
What do you think about Riley’s claim that engineers are “unlikely to question authority”?
Assignment (Thursday in class): be prepared for a short writing assignment based the reading (hardcopy supplied in class). Reading in advance and thinking about the questions above will help you do well on assignment.

Reading Overview, week 2

Reading: Michael Davis, “An Historical Preface to Engineering Ethics,” Science and Engineering Ethics 1, no. 1 (1995): 33–48; Henry Petroski, “Scientists vs. Engineers,” in An Engineer’s Alphabet: Gleanings from the Softer Side of a Profession (Cambridge University Press, 2011), 280-283.

Overview: There are two readings from today. The first is from philosopher Michael Davis, who is a leading authority on engineering ethics. The second is by the late Henry Petroski, a professor of Civil Engineering at Duke who wrote extensively about engineering and its history. The Davis reading focuses on using history to explore the meanings of key terms. You can skim the sections on “Ethic” and “Values,” as we’ll be focusing on these issues later.

Questions to think about while reading:

Why, according to Davis, does “science” usually come first in the phrases “science and technology” and “science and engineering”?
People who do science are called “scientists.” Is there a comparable term for people who do technology? Is this term (or terms) adequate?
How old is the profession of engineering, according to Davis?
What the difference between science and engineering, according to Davis and Petroski? Do they agree or disagree on this question?
Assignment (Thursday): we’ll have a short-answer quiz in response to the questions above (one or two sentence answers). It will be closed book.
